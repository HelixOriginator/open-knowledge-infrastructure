The Strategic Context

The Global South Knowledge Gap

The Systemic Bottleneck: In India and the broader Global South, high-impact research on digital public infrastructure, AI governance, and public policy is disproportionately gatekept by resource-heavy, traditional academic institutions. This creates a critical blind spot, limiting agile, grassroots-informed perspectives in global knowledge systems.

The Proven Alternative: This initiative demonstrates that rigorous, large-scale, and globally indexed scholarship can be produced independently. By leveraging open-access repositories and persistent identifiers (DOIs), we have built a resilient knowledge infrastructure that operates without institutional overhead or paywalls.

The Imperative: This is not a concept-stage proposal. It is a living, publicly accessible ecosystem. Strategic funding is required not to prove the model, but to preserve it from decay and transition it into a sustainable, institutionalized public resource.

Continuity Risk

The Fragility of Independent Open Knowledge

Why preserving this ecosystem is a time-sensitive, public-interest priority.

Key Person Dependency

Currently, the entire infrastructure is maintained by a single architect. Without funding to build a small support team or transition to an institutional host, the ecosystem is highly vulnerable to stagnation or abrupt cessation.

Infrastructure Decay

Maintaining DOI registrations, updating dataset metadata, and ensuring long-term repository compliance requires ongoing, dedicated resources that cannot be sustained indefinitely on a voluntary, self-funded basis.

Loss to the Public Domain

Halting this work means the Global South loses a proven, agile model for independent knowledge production, reinforcing the monopoly of traditional, well-funded academic institutions and reducing the diversity of global scholarship.

Funding Priorities

Institutionalization Roadmap

A phased, transparent approach to transitioning from a founder-led initiative to a sustainable public institution.

Phase 1: Stabilization (Months 1-6)

Secure & Formalize

Establish fiscal sponsorship or register as a Section 8 (nonprofit) entity in India. Audit all existing DOI metadata for long-term compliance and establish formal, foundation-grade data governance protocols.

Phase 2: Scaling (Months 7-18)

Expand Capacity

Resource a small, specialized team (research assistants, data curators) to increase the velocity of high-quality output, develop open-source research tooling, and actively disseminate findings to policymakers.

Phase 3: Institutionalization (Months 19-36)

Perpetual Continuity

Forge a formal hosting partnership with an established open-knowledge foundation (e.g., Wikimedia, OSF) or operate as an independent, sustainably funded research trust, ensuring the work outlives the founder.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is this different from a concept-stage research proposal?
This is not a proposal to build something new. The infrastructure, taxonomy, and 250+ DOI-linked outputs already exist and are publicly verifiable. Funding is sought to preserve, scale, and institutionalize an already proven, functioning model.
How will funded resources be governed and ensured for public benefit?
All current and future outputs are strictly Open Access under Creative Commons (CC-BY) licenses. Future funding agreements will include explicit covenants ensuring that all newly produced research, datasets, and frameworks remain permanently in the public domain.
What is the legal structure for receiving institutional funding?
The initiative is currently structured to accept funding via fiscal sponsorship or is in the process of formal registration as a Section 8 (nonprofit) company in India, ensuring full compliance with domestic and international grant-making regulations.
Can funders see the existing outputs for verification?
Absolutely. A comprehensive Due Diligence Packet, including a verified index of all DOI-linked outputs, repository links, the Kallol Taxonomy framework, and a detailed budget projection, is available upon request for serious institutional inquiries.
